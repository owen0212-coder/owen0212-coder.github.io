02 / DCF WORKBENCH

What’s it actually worth?

Runs in your browser

A simple unlevered free-cash-flow valuation. Change the assumptions, see the consequences. The starting numbers are fictional—not a valuation of a listed company.

Your assumptions

Money in USD millions; shares in millions. Share prices are USD.

FCFF is cash available to debt and equity holders, before debt payments. Use normalized unlevered cash flow—not net income or levered FCF.

Assumptions save in this browser only.

Open the hood: methodology & limitations

FCFF grows at your annual rate through the explicit forecast. Each year’s cash flow is discounted at a constant WACC using year-end timing. Terminal value assumes perpetual growth after the last forecast year.

Enterprise value = Σ FCFFₜ / (1 + WACC)ᵗ + [FCFFₙ × (1 + g) / (WACC − g)] / (1 + WACC)ⁿ

Equity value = enterprise value + excess cash − debt and other senior claims. Value per share = equity value ÷ diluted shares. Include preferred stock, non-controlling interests, or other relevant claims in the debt/claims input as appropriate; avoid double-counting leases.

This simplified model assumes positive normalized FCFF, a stable capital structure, and a sustainable terminal growth rate. It does not separately model revenue, margins, taxes, reinvestment, future dilution, or stock-based compensation. It is unsuitable for banks or businesses with negative cash flow without a more detailed model. Negative equity outputs are left visible, not presented as literal negative stock prices.

Methodology reference: Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern: firm valuation ↗

03 / LBO DEAL LAB

Buy. Build. Pay it down.

No spreadsheet required

What makes a buyout work: growth, a better exit, or less debt? Model a leveraged acquisition and follow the money. All starting assumptions are fictional, not current financing terms. Jump to returns ↓

Structure the deal

Money in USD millions. Multiples use EBITDA. A single debt tranche, with no interim distributions.

01 / The business

Margin stays constant, so EBITDA grows at the same rate as revenue.

02 / Entry & exit

Cash-free, debt-free purchase. Entry fees include transaction and financing costs.

03 / The financing

Amortization is a % of original debt. The sweep repays debt using available cash after mandatory payments. Interest uses opening debt.

04 / Cash conversion

NWC is the operating working-capital balance; only its annual change consumes or releases cash.

Assumptions save in this browser only.

Open the hood: LBO methodology & limitations

Purchase enterprise value = base-year EBITDA × entry multiple. Uses equal purchase EV plus entry fees; sources are new debt and sponsor equity. The business is delivered cash-free and debt-free with normal operating working capital included in the price. Opening excess cash is zero; no separate minimum cash reserve is funded.

Revenue grows annually at a constant rate and margin remains fixed. Cash taxes equal max(EBIT − cash interest, 0) × tax rate. Interest is fully deductible in this simplified model; tax-law limits, loss carryforwards, purchase accounting and fee tax shields are not modeled. D&A reduces taxable income but is non-cash.

Cash flow before principal = EBITDA − interest − cash taxes − capex − change in NWC

Interest uses beginning-of-year debt; principal is paid at year-end. Mandatory repayment is capped at remaining debt, then the cash sweep applies to all remaining available cash, including prior retained cash. Unused cash earns no interest and is retained until exit. A cash or mandatory-payment shortfall stops the forecast and suppresses returns: there is no assumed revolver or rescue equity. Remaining debt is repaid at exit; no earlier maturity or covenants are modeled.

Exit proceeds = max(0, exit EBITDA × exit multiple − exit fees − debt + excess cash)
MOIC = exit proceeds ÷ initial sponsor equity
Annual IRR = MOIC^(1 / holding years) − 1

That IRR formula is exact here because there is one initial equity outflow and one exit inflow, with no interim dividends or extra contributions. A total equity loss displays −100%. Debt exceeding net exit assets is flagged separately, not treated as negative sponsor proceeds. Returns are gross deal-level figures before fund fees, carry and investor taxes. Sensitivities change one operating-growth rate and the exit multiple, holding other assumptions fixed.

This is a study and screening model, not an investment-committee-ready three-statement model. It excludes multiple debt tranches, floating-rate curves, PIK, refinancing, acquisitions, management dilution and distributions. Educational reference: Wall Street Prep: LBO modeling mechanics ↗.
